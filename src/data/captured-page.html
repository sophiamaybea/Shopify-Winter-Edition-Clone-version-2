Method

Study the craft. Try it on the page. Find out what happened. Revise with purpose. Finish something real.

See what the writing is doing

Close reading

Instead of memorising rules, look closely at an actual craft decision. Where does the poem change pressure? What does the line break withhold? Why does one physical detail carry more feeling than a paragraph of explanation?

Diagnosis before editing

Before changing a draft, work out what the problem actually is. Find the live material, the dead patch, the unnecessary explanation, the structural hesitation or the sentence doing more work than everything around it.

Path

You do not need to know what kind of writer you are yet. Start with what you want to make or what keeps going wrong.

Find Your Writing Path

You might be starting from nothing. You might have twenty unfinished poems. You might write constantly but distrust your endings. You might have a draft you have revised into complete immobility. Choose by the problem in front of you rather than an arbitrary idea of “beginner” or “advanced”.
